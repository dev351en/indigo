### Editorial & Body Copy

> We are continuing to build and improve our list of Accolades. Please contact us with suggested additions or corrections.

> In 1938, an anonymous gift to the University created an endowment, the purpose of which was unique in American higher education: a monetary award to be presented annually to faculty members in recognition of their excellence in teaching undergraduate students. The donor&rsquo;s anonymity technically prevailed for more than a decade, but in 1952, University Trustee&nbsp;Ernest Quantrell&nbsp;added to his gift, consented to be acknowledged as the donor and named the award for his parents,&nbsp;Llewellyn John&nbsp;and&nbsp;Harriet Manchester Quantrell.

> The Quantrell Award is believed to be the nation&rsquo;s oldest prize for undergraduate teaching. Based on letters of nomination from students, the award is among the most treasured by faculty. Nobel Laureate&nbsp;James Cronin, University Professor in Physics, said he was &ldquo;bowled over to be receiving this Quantrell prize.&rdquo;

> &ldquo;I really care about teaching undergraduates&mdash;it&rsquo;s both a great responsibility and a great pleasure&mdash;and to have this recognition is just incredible. It&rsquo;s better than some of the more public awards I&rsquo;ve gotten,&rdquo; he said.

> In a 1952 issue of the&nbsp;University of Chicago Magazine, Quantrell described the motivation behind his gift. &ldquo;The success of a university depends on its product, and its product consists of students trained to lead happy, proficient, useful and unselfish lives. To obtain this product, a good faculty is essential and constitutes the most important part of a university. We have had, and still have, great teachers, but we will have still greater ones.&rdquo;
