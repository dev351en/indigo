### Editorial & Body Copy

> Since its founding in 1890, UChicago scholars have made research discoveries that have transformed our world. From fields ranging from astronomy to immunology and economics to sociology, members of the UChicago community have changed the way we live and think. Trace the history of their breakthrough research throughout the years in the timeline below.

> UChicago’s Sociology Department was the first in the U.S. Its graduates and faculty have shaped sociological subfields and originated methodologies widely used today.

> Philosopher and educator John Dewey opened an elementary school in 1896, embarking on one of the most important educational experiments of the century. That small school became the University of Chicago Laboratory Schools, one of the preeminent pre-collegiate academic institutions in the world.&nbsp;

> Botanist Henry Chandler Cowles pioneered the concept of ecological succession as he studied plant communities along the shores of Lake Michigan in the nearby Indiana Dunes. He is considered one of the founders of modern ecology.

> Prof. George Herbert Mead is regarded as one of the founders of the American sociological tradition, particularly the school known as Symbolic Interactionism, as well as the philosophical paradigm known as Pragmatism. His work includes theories of the mind and how the human self develops through social interaction.

> Prof. Albert A. Michelson made groundbreaking measurements of the speed of light&mdash;providing support for Einstein&rsquo;s theory of relativity. In 1907, Michelson became the first scientist from the United States to win a Nobel Prize.

> The School of Social Service Administration (now known as the Crown Family School of Social Work, Policy and Practice) was among the first schools of social work in the U.S. and a pioneer in using scientific research to identify and address the root causes of social inequity. Today, Crown Family School alumni lead public and private welfare agencies around the world.

> Prof. James Henry Breasted argued that the roots of Western civilization lay in the Middle East, rather than Greek and Roman cultures. He coined the term 'Fertile Crescent' for the area where Mesopotamian cultures flourished millennia ago.

> Photo via Hanna Holborn Gray Special Collections Research Center

> Oswald Robertson discovered a way to preserve blood and pioneered the idea of blood banks, which he established during World War I with the U.S. Army Medical Corps.

> UChicago sociologists William I. Thomas and Florian Witold Znaniecki won international acclaim as co-authors of The Polish Peasant in Europe and America (1918&ndash;20), today considered the foundation of modern empirical sociology.

> The Chicago Assyrian Dictionary, a 90-year project begun in 1921 and completed in 2011, collected a wealth of information about ancient Near Eastern civilization. In essence a cultural encyclopedia of ancient Mesopotamian history, it is one of the most important contributions to knowledge of ancient Near Eastern civilizations.

> In 1921, political scientist Charles Merriam outlined an alternate vision for political science, founded on behavioralism. This became the Chicago School of Political Science, which reconceived the study of politics as a scientific endeavor on the model of the natural sciences.

> In 1923, UChicago physiologist Arno B. Luckhardt discovered the anesthetic use of ethylene gas. In the 20 years following his discovery, ethylene came into general use as an anesthetic in major operations.

> Prof. James Henry Breasted developed a technique to record the historic texts carved into temples and tombs without damaging them. Today known as the Chicago House Method, the process blends photography, illustration and careful eyes to create an exact copy. For over 100 years, the Epigraphic Survey team has traveled to Luxor, Egypt each fall to precisely document this ancient human history.
