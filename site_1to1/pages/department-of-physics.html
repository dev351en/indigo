Carrying Forward the Legacy of Fermi, Michelson, and Chandrasekhar

The Department of Physics at the University of Chicago is one of the most storied institutions in the history of science. From Albert Michelson's pioneering speed-of-light measurements to Enrico Fermi's first controlled nuclear chain reaction (Chicago Pile-1), UChicago physicists have repeatedly reshaped our understanding of the universe across high-energy particle physics, theoretical cosmology, condensed matter, and quantum optics.

Department Highlights

  • 30 Nobel Laureates in Physics affiliated with the department
  • Historic home to Enrico Fermi, Albert Michelson, Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar, and James Cronin
  • Direct leadership in major international experiments: CERN (ATLAS), Fermilab (DUNE), South Pole Telescope, and James Webb Space Telescope
  • Close affiliation with the Enrico Fermi Institute (EFI) and James Franck Institute (JFI)

Research Areas & Specializations

Faculty, postdoctoral fellows, and graduate researchers collaborate across interdisciplinary fields, driving theoretical innovations and empirical breakthroughs.

Particle Physics & Fields

Collider experiments at CERN LHC, neutrino oscillations with Fermilab DUNE, dark matter detection, and fundamental quantum field theory.

Astrophysics & Cosmology

Cosmic microwave background observations (South Pole Telescope), dark energy surveys, cosmic ray physics, and cosmological evolution.

Condensed Matter & Materials Physics

Quantum materials, high-temperature superconductivity, topological insulators, non-equilibrium systems, and soft matter.

Atomic, Molecular & Optical (AMO) Physics

Ultracold quantum gases, optical lattices, Bose-Einstein condensation, quantum simulation, and precision measurement.

Theoretical Physics & String Theory

Quantum gravity, holographic duality (AdS/CFT), strongly coupled quantum fluids, and relativistic hydrodynamics.

World-Class Facilities & Affiliated Institutes

  • Michelson Center for Physics advanced cleanrooms and optics laboratories
  • Enrico Fermi Institute (EFI) machine shops and radiation detectors
  • James Franck Institute (JFI) low-temperature cryostat facilities
  • Fermilab and Argonne National Laboratory high-energy beamlines
