Critical Rigor, Literary Imagination, and Aesthetic Interpretation

The Department of English Language and Literature at the University of Chicago is renowned for visionary scholarship in literary history, critical theory, cultural studies, and creative writing. Our faculty pioneer influential approaches in affect theory, media aesthetics, early modern drama, American literature, and postcolonial poetics.

Department Highlights

  • Home to Guggenheim Fellows, Pulitzer Prize winners, and leading literary theorists
  • Pioneered critical affect theory and visual culture studies (Critical Inquiry journal)
  • Rich ties to the Chicago Review and the Committee on Creative Writing
  • Renowned doctoral program with comprehensive funding and international archive access

Academic Degree Programs

Undergraduate Program in English Language & Literature

The B.A. in English provides deep textual analysis, historical interpretation, and creative expression, offering courses from medieval romance to contemporary global fiction.

Degrees Conferred: Bachelor of Arts (B.A.), Bachelor of Science (B.S.) • Administered through The College

Master’s & Professional Degree Curricula

Master of Arts Program in the Humanities (MAPH) with English concentration.

Doctor of Philosophy (Ph.D.) Program

Full Fellowship Funding

The Ph.D. in English trains scholars and teachers of literature, supported by 5-year full fellowship packages covering tuition, stipend, and research allowances.

Guaranteed 5-Year Doctoral Fellowship Package:
  • 100% Full Tuition Remission covered by the University
  • Competitive Annual Living Stipend ($44,000+ per year)
  • Comprehensive UChicago Student Health Insurance (U-SHIP)
  • Guaranteed research assistantship and travel conference grants

Research Areas & Specializations

Faculty, postdoctoral fellows, and graduate researchers collaborate across interdisciplinary fields, driving theoretical innovations and empirical breakthroughs.

Critical Theory & Cultural Studies

Aesthetic theory, affect theory, political criticism, and cultural studies.

Early Modern & Renaissance Literature

Shakespeare, early modern drama, poetry, religious culture, and print history.

American Literature & Modernism

19th- and 20th-century American fiction, African American literature, poetics, and Cold War culture.

Media Aesthetics & Visual Culture

Word-and-image relations, cinematic adaptation, digital humanities, and publication history.

World-Class Facilities & Affiliated Institutes

  • Walker Museum Building departmental seminars and student reading rooms
  • Critical Inquiry editorial suites
  • Special Collections Research Center at Regenstein Library
